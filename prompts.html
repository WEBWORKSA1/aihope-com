AI prompts for work and careers

30 prompts written for real situations: checking your career, finding a job, learning faster, writing clearly and making decisions. Replace the [brackets], paste, and go.

Career check-up

Map my job's AI exposure

I work as a [job title] in [industry]. List the 10 tasks that take most of my week, estimate what share of my time each takes, and for each one say whether AI can already do it, help me do it faster, or barely touch it. Finish with the three tasks I should hand to AI first and the three I should invest in getting better at.

Career check-up

Find adjacent careers

My current role is [job title] and my strongest skills are [skills]. Suggest 5 adjacent roles that use most of these skills but are less exposed to AI automation. For each, list the skill gap, a realistic timeline to close it and one free way to start this week.

Career check-up

Stress-test a career move

I'm considering moving from [current role] to [target role]. Argue against this move as strongly as you honestly can, then argue for it. End with the three questions I should answer before deciding and who I could ask.

The anatomy of a good prompt

The best prompts read like a good brief to a smart new colleague. They usually include five things:

  1. Context: who you are, what the situation is and why it matters.
  2. The task: exactly what you want done, in one or two sentences.
  3. Input: the material to work from, pasted in full or attached.
  4. Format: the shape of the answer: a table, five bullets, 200 words, an email.
  5. Guardrails: what to avoid, such as inventing facts, plus a request to ask you questions when something is missing.
Tip: when the answer is not right, don't start over. Tell the AI what to change: “Shorter, more direct, and lead with the deadline.” Iterating is faster than re-prompting.

Always check the output

AI assistants can state wrong facts confidently. Verify numbers, names, quotes, dates and anything legal, medical or financial against a reliable source before you use it. The prompt “Check AI output for errors” above is a useful second pass, but it does not replace your own judgment.

Do these prompts work with any AI assistant?

Yes. They are written in plain language and work with ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and most other assistants. Results vary slightly between tools.

Is it okay to use AI for my resume and cover letter?

Yes, as long as everything stays true and sounds like you. Use AI to structure and tighten your own facts, never to invent experience. Many recruiters can spot generic AI writing, so always edit the result in your own voice.
