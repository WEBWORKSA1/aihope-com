Hope stories

Hope stories: AI doing real good

Fear makes headlines. These are the quieter stories of people using AI to cure, teach, protect and include. Each one links to the organization behind it so you can read more.

Science

A map of life's building blocks, free for all

Working out a protein's 3D shape used to take years of lab work. Google DeepMind's AlphaFold, with EMBL's European Bioinformatics Institute, made predicted structures for hundreds of millions of proteins freely available. Researchers use them to study diseases, design enzymes and develop vaccines. AlphaFold's creators shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

Why it matters for work: AI did not replace biologists. It gave them a head start and opened new research jobs.

Accessibility

An assistant that sees for people who can't

Be My Eyes connects blind and low-vision people with sighted volunteers. Its AI assistant now describes photos, reads labels and menus, and helps with everyday tasks at any hour, while human volunteers remain available when people want them.

Why it matters for work: AI can widen access to jobs and independence for people with disabilities.

Climate

Earlier warnings before rivers flood

Floods are among the most common and deadly natural disasters. Google's AI flood forecasting models predict river floods days in advance and publish alerts through Flood Hub across many countries, including regions with little local monitoring data.

Why it matters for work: climate adaptation is creating jobs that combine data skills with local knowledge.

Google Flood Hub ↗
Education

A patient tutor for every student

Khan Academy, a nonprofit, built Khanmigo, an AI tutor that guides students toward answers instead of handing them out, and helps teachers plan lessons and give feedback. Sal Khan argues AI could bring personal tutoring, once a luxury, to every learner.

Why it matters for work: teachers who use AI well spend more time teaching and less on paperwork.

Khanmigo ↗
Health

Catching eye disease before it blinds

Diabetic retinopathy can cause blindness if it is not caught early, and many regions lack enough specialists to screen everyone. AI screening systems that read retinal photos have been deployed in clinics in India and Thailand, helping nurses and doctors find patients who need treatment sooner.

Why it matters for work: AI extends what each clinician can do; it does not remove the need for care.

Nature

Old phones that listen for chainsaws

Rainforest Connection recycles old smartphones into solar-powered listening devices placed in forest canopies. AI analyzes the sound in real time to detect chainsaws and logging trucks, and alerts rangers so they can act, while also helping scientists monitor wildlife.

Why it matters for work: conservation increasingly needs people who can work with sensors and data.

Rainforest Connection ↗
Inclusion

Translation for languages the internet forgot

Most online content exists in a handful of languages. Meta's No Language Left Behind project released open translation models covering around 200 languages, including many with very little digital text, so researchers and nonprofits can build tools for communities that were left out.

Why it matters for work: local language experts are essential to building and checking these tools.

No Language Left Behind ↗
Fairness

Holding AI to account

Hope also means honesty about harm. Researcher Joy Buolamwini showed that commercial face-analysis systems performed worst on darker-skinned women, and founded the Algorithmic Justice League to push for fairer, more accountable AI. Her work changed how companies test their systems.

Why it matters for work: AI auditing, policy and safety are fast-growing fields.
