What the patterns tell us

Across the 38 jobs we score, three patterns stand out. First, office and information work is the most exposed: administrative, bookkeeping, translation and paralegal roles are dominated by reading, writing and processing information, which is exactly what large language models do well. This matches the International Labour Organization's 2025 finding that clerical work remains the most exposed occupational group.

Second, hands-on and care work is the least exposed. Caregivers, construction workers, electricians and nurses spend most of their time on physical, unpredictable or relationship-based tasks. The World Economic Forum also expects some of these roles, including farmworkers, construction workers and nurses, to add the most jobs in absolute numbers this decade.

Third, exposure and outlook are different things. Software developers and data analysts are highly exposed, yet demand for them keeps growing, because AI makes each person more productive and creates new work. The people at risk are those who keep doing exposed tasks the old way.
