Will AI replace lawyers?

Research and first drafts are accelerating, but advice, negotiation and courtroom advocacy depend on accountability that AI cannot carry. The practical risk for lawyers is relying on AI output that has not been checked.

The short answer

AI can take over or speed up a meaningful slice of the week for lawyers, but the core of the job still depends on human judgment, relationships or physical work. The score is an estimate of how much of the typical working week involves tasks that current AI tools can perform or speed up. It is not a probability that the job disappears. Read how we calculate it.

“Can automate” means AI can increasingly do the task with light human review. “Assists” means AI makes a person faster or better, but a human still does the work and owns the result.

What this means for you

AI is most likely to take over legal research and drafting contracts and filings. If those tasks fill most of your days, start handing them to AI tools now, on your terms, and use the time you save to grow the parts of the job that stay human.

Your most durable work is negotiation and advocacy and advising clients. These depend on trust, physical presence, context or accountability, which is why employers keep paying for them even as tools improve.

Job outlook

Stable Demand looks broadly stable, while the tasks inside the job change. Firms are shifting from billable hours on routine drafting toward fixed-fee, higher-judgment work.

Frequently asked questions

Will AI replace lawyers?

Not entirely. On AI Hope's task-based estimate, this job scores 38/100 for AI exposure (moderate exposure). Demand looks broadly stable, while the tasks inside the job change. The biggest changes are in legal research and drafting contracts and filings.

Which tasks can AI already do for lawyers?

AI is strongest at legal research (exposure 65/100) and drafting contracts and filings (exposure 60/100). It has the least impact on negotiation and advocacy and advising clients.

What should lawyers learn to stay ahead of AI?

Focus on verifying AI-generated research, aI governance and technology law, negotiation and advocacy and pricing and delivering legal work efficiently.
