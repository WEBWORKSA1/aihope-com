Path 1 · Beginner · about 20 hours

Start with AI

For anyone who wants to understand what AI is, what it can and cannot do, and how to use it responsibly. No maths or coding.

Week 1–2

Take Elements of AI to understand the basics: what machine learning is, where it fails and why it matters.

Week 3

Do AI Fluency to learn how to work with AI assistants: delegating, describing tasks, checking output and using it ethically.

Week 4

Use an AI assistant for one real task every day. Keep notes on what worked. Earn an IBM SkillsBuild credential.

How to actually finish a course

Most online courses are never completed. These habits make the difference:

  • Learn on a schedule, not on willpower. Put two or three fixed sessions in your calendar each week.
  • Apply everything to your own work. After each lesson, try the idea on a real task the same day.
  • Make something visible. A small project, a before-and-after example or a short write-up counts for more with employers than a certificate alone.
  • Learn with others. A colleague, a study group or the AI Hope Challenge keeps you going.

Free vs. paid certificates

Free courses teach the same ideas as paid ones. Paid certificates can help when an employer asks for proof, or when a course includes graded projects and support. A practical approach: learn free first, then pay only for the certificate that your target employers actually mention in job posts.

Which free AI course should I take first?

Elements of AI from the University of Helsinki is the best general starting point for non-technical learners. If you already use AI assistants, Anthropic's AI Fluency course is a short, practical next step.

Do I need to learn to code to work with AI?

No. Most jobs need AI literacy, not programming: knowing how to give AI good instructions, check its output and fit it into your workflow. Coding becomes important if you want to build AI products or move into technical roles.

How long does it take to become good at using AI at work?

With daily practice, most people become noticeably more productive within four to six weeks. Becoming the go-to AI person on a team usually takes two to three months of deliberate use and sharing what you learn.
