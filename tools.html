How to choose an AI tool

  1. Start from the task, not the tool. Write down the three tasks that eat most of your week. Pick tools for those only.
  2. Prefer tools inside software you already use. Copilot in Microsoft 365 or Gemini in Google Workspace often beats a new app you have to remember to open.
  3. Check the data rules. Before pasting work documents, read your employer's AI policy and the tool's privacy terms. Never paste passwords, customer personal data or confidential numbers into a tool your organization has not approved.
  4. Test for two weeks, then decide. Measure time saved and quality. Keep what works and drop the rest.

A starter kit for most jobs

One general assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot), one meeting notetaker (Otter.ai, Fireflies.ai or Fathom), one research tool with sources (Perplexity or NotebookLM) and one automation tool (Zapier or Make). That combination covers writing, meetings, research and repetitive admin, which is where most people save the most time.

Which AI tool is best for beginners?

Any of the major assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Microsoft Copilot) is a good start. Pick the one that fits the software you already use, and spend your energy learning to prompt well rather than switching tools.

Are free AI tools safe to use at work?

It depends on your employer's rules and the data involved. Free consumer plans may use your inputs to improve their models unless you change the settings. Use approved business versions for confidential information.
